# Design panel: summary

Produced 2026-11-02 by the architecture workflow (3 proposals → 3 judges → synthesis → 2 skeptics → revision). Panel output: [architecture-panel.md](architecture-panel.md), [plan-panel.md](plan-panel.md). The reconciled, authoritative documents are `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/PLAN.md`.

## Proposals

- Cutscene v1: fastest robust recorder (platform-does-the-work plan) (Fastest robust v1: shortest path to a recorder the team relies on within days. Let ScreenCaptureKit, AVFoundation and the composition engine do the heavy lifting (SCRecordingOutput for capture, AVVideoCompositing for preview and export) while keeping effects as render-time metadata. Cut everything not needed for v1 but leave typed plug-in points for code/terminal effects, transcript editing and brand kits.)
- Cutscene v1 architecture: quality and performance ceiling (Quality and performance ceiling: frame-accurate sync, 4K60 capture without drops, a pristine intermediate, color-correct output, a Metal pipeline that scales to motion blur and later effects, fast exports. More code is accepted where it buys measurable quality; each such choice states its cost in engineer-days.)
- Cutscene v1 architecture: a non-destructive, kit-driven recorder whose data model already carries the roadmap (Editability and future-proofing: every user-changeable thing is versioned data; media is immutable; preview and export are one function of that data; brand kits, transcripts and code-aware effects have homes in the v1 schema.)

## Judges

- Winner: Cutscene v1: fastest robust recorder (platform-does-the-work plan) — totals: Cutscene v1: fastest robust recorder (platform-does-the-work plan): 39, Cutscene v1 architecture: quality and performance ceiling: 37, Cutscene v1 architecture: a non-destructive, kit-driven recorder whose data model already carries the roadmap: 35
  All three proposals are feasible on macOS 15 under Swift 6 strict concurrency; I found no wrong API or wrong minimum-OS claim in any of them after grepping roughly fifty declarations across ScreenCaptureKit, AVFoundation, VideoToolbox, CoreMedia, AppKit, CoreGraphics, Carbon and the Swift module interfaces (notable confirmations: sourceReadOnlyPixelBuffer(byTrackID:) is macOS 26.0 only, Synchronization.Mutex is 15.0, DispatchSerialQueue.asUnownedSerialExecutor() is 14.0, streamDidBecomeInactive is 15.2, AVVideoCompressionPropertiesKey accepts VideoToolbox keys, AVVideoAverageBitRateKey is annotated 'H.264 only', startSessionAtSourceTime edits out earlier samples and inserts empty edits for late tracks, and RegisterEventHotKey has no header in the CLT SDK). The errors are in sync reasoning and build plumbing, not APIs. P1 wins (39) because it gets the team recording in days with the least code on the hot paths, its concurrency model is the cleanest to implement, and its risks come with time-boxed spikes and pre-wired fallbacks; its one real flaw is treating the SCRecordingOutput file-time offset as a constant, which must become a per-recording derivation or a switch to SampleWriter with startSession(at: t0). P2 (37) has the soundest clock model and the highest ceiling but overstates what AVVideoComposition cannot do and underestimates its own preview player, delaying styled output by two to three weeks. P3 (35) has the best data model for the three differentiators and a strong zero-hop capture actor, but asserts unverified CAF crash safety, risks a SwiftPM .metal compile step without Xcode, and front-loads reducer/undo scope that delays the first real video.

Graft into the winning design, from P2: (1) control HEVC and H.264 rate with VideoToolbox keys inside AVVideoCompressionPropertiesKey (kVTCompressionPropertyKey_AverageBitRate, DataRateLimits, MaxKeyFrameIntervalDuration, MaxAllowedFrameQP 12.0) plus AVVideoEncoderSpecificationKey with RequireHardwareAcceleratedVideoEncoder, which deletes P1's S5 bitrate question and gives the SampleWriter fallback real quality control; (2) convert SCK PTS with CMSyncConvertTime(from: stream.synchronizationClock, to: host) instead of assuming identity; (3) store SCStreamFrameInfoDisplayTime per frame (via CMClockMakeHostTimeFromSystemUnits) so clicks align with what was on screen; (4) ProcessInfo.beginActivity([.userInitiated, .idleDisplaySleepDisabled, .latencyCritical]) for the recording's lifetime; (5) dropped-frame telemetry in the manifest and HUD; (6) click anchoring (blend the smoothed cursor to the raw click point over ±120 ms) and the jitter gate; (7) the pixel-perfect zoom level rule and log-size viewport interpolation, plus viewportVelocity in FrameState for later motion blur; (8) correct platform-notes.md (queueDepth default and max 8, xf44/RGhA, kCMTimeZero native refresh, HDR plus recording output fails, streamDidBecomeInactive 15.2); (9) lenient decoding that preserves unknown annotation kinds as raw JSON. From P3: (10) start every own writer session at a shared t0 so media time 0 equals event time 0, and for SCRecordingOutput derive the offset per recording from the written video track's first sample; (11) the paused re-render bypass (cache last source frames in the compositor and redraw directly to the preview layer on slider changes, which implies AVPlayerItemVideoOutput plus an own CAMetalLayer rather than AVPlayerLayer); (12) DispatchSerialQueue as the capture actor's executor shared with sampleHandlerQueue plus assumeIsolated via an NSObject shim; (13) reserved event kinds transcriptSegment, codeContext and terminalCommand with .unknown(kind, raw) round-trip, and appFocus plus windowGeometry recorded in v1; (14) Cut with provenance and ZoomSegment.locked in the Timeline types now, with the reducer and undo deferred; (15) a local NSEvent monitor for clicks on our own windows, since the header says global monitors skip own-app events; (16) SCStreamFrameInfoScreenRect (13.1) alongside ContentRect for window-mode mapping; (17) keep sharingType = .none off and rely on the application filter, per the header's system-services caution.
- Winner: Cutscene v1: fastest robust recorder (platform-does-the-work plan) — totals: Cutscene v1: fastest robust recorder (platform-does-the-work plan): 42, Cutscene v1 architecture: quality and performance ceiling: 36, Cutscene v1 architecture: a non-destructive, kit-driven recorder whose data model already carries the roadmap: 39
  From the seat of a DevRel engineer who wants to record a demo today, Proposal 1 wins (42) over Proposal 3 (39) and Proposal 2 (36). It is the only plan whose capture path owns no pixel or encoder code (SCRecordingOutput plus a passive .screen output, the exact combination already compiled in the repo's RecordingSpike.swift), whose preview and export are one AVVideoCompositing compositor so preview equals export by construction, and whose only TCC permission is Screen Recording. Every header claim it marked verified held up in the CLT SDK, including the macOS 26.0-only replacement for sourceFrame(byTrackID:), and each of its five spikes has a decision variable and a drop-in fallback (SampleWriter with 2 s fragments; own decode loop). Its mistakes are small: a spike cross-reference typo, stamping input events on main-thread arrival instead of NSEvent.timestamp, keeping sharingType = .none despite NSWindow.h's caution, and day labels about 2x optimistic. Proposal 2 has the highest quality ceiling and an accurate API inventory but reaches a styled export roughly three times later, overstates MaxAllowedFrameQP support (the header says it is not supported in all encoders), pairs 'xf44' 4:4:4 capture with a 4:2:2 HEVC profile, and costs its own preview player at 3 days with no fallback. Proposal 3 has the best data model but its capture concurrency story contradicts itself (separate MediaFileWriter actors receiving non-Sendable CMSampleBuffers despite a 'zero-hop' claim), asserts CAF crash-safety unverified, and relies on SCContentFilter.includedWindows (15.2) on a 15.0 floor. The final design should be Proposal 1's pipeline carrying Proposal 3's schema (Cuts with provenance, unknown-kind round-tripping, kit snapshot plus sparse overrides) and Proposal 2's cheap quality wins (per-frame display time, click-anchored zero-phase smoothing, pixel-perfect zoom level, latency-critical activity assertion, dropped-frame telemetry). One practical note: ~/Movies/Cutscene is empty, so SCRecordingOutput has never run on this machine; spike S1 (crash-safety and movie-time-zero alignment, with fileTimeOffset read from the written asset's video track start) should literally be the first task.
- Winner: Cutscene v1: fastest robust recorder (platform-does-the-work plan) — totals: Cutscene v1: fastest robust recorder (platform-does-the-work plan): 41, Cutscene v1 architecture: quality and performance ceiling: 37, Cutscene v1 architecture: a non-destructive, kit-driven recorder whose data model already carries the roadmap: 37
  Winner: Proposal 1 (fastest robust recorder), total 41 versus 37 for each of the other two. From a maintainability and open-source lens it owns the least code on the hot paths (SCRecordingOutput for capture, one AVVideoCompositing compositor for preview and export), builds directly on the SCRecordingOutput spike that already runs in Sources/CutsceneCapture/RecordingSpike.swift, keeps Core Foundation-only with human-readable JSON/JSONL fixtures, exposes the tightest pure Engine API for trace-based tests, maps modules to files concretely, and is the only proposal whose Swift 6 concurrency plan is consistent with CMSampleBufferRef and CVPixelBufferRef being @_nonSendable in this SDK (CMSampleBuffer.h:132, CVPixelBuffer.h:350). Its SCRecordingOutput claims all verified against SCRecordingOutput.h and SCStream.h:518-531 (including that a configuration update stops the recording and HDR recording fails). Its weaknesses are a schema inconsistency in the zoom track, no undo story, Double-second timestamps and no per-frame index; all are fixed by grafts. Proposal 2 has the highest quality ceiling (display-time alignment, pixel-perfect zoom, VideoToolbox rate-control keys that sidestep the 'H.264 only' annotation at AVVideoSettings.h:201-204, cutscene-tool) but owns the most code, has a concrete strict-concurrency error (queue.async with a non-Sendable CMSampleBuffer) and reaches v1 in about five weeks. Proposal 3 has the best data model for the roadmap (ProjectEdit reducer, Cut provenance, StyleOverrides, unknown-kind round-tripping, reserved event shapes, CaptureSink protocol, Shaders.metal as a resource, which I verified builds under the Command Line Tools) but contradicts itself on concurrency (actor-per-file writer receiving non-Sendable buffers, actor hop in startRequest), adds a second preview display path without a spike, and carries v1 abstractions that delay a styled export to about day 18. Other verified facts used in scoring: RegisterEventHotKey and IsSecureEventInputEnabled are exported by HIToolbox.tbd but declared in no header of the Command Line Tools SDK (all three were right); DispatchSerialQueue is a SerialExecutor from macOS 14; Synchronization.Mutex is macOS 15; streamDidBecomeInactive is 15.2 and needs an availability gate; microphoneCaptureDeviceID is the AVCaptureDevice uniqueID (SCStream.h:363). The final design should be Proposal 1 with the grafts listed under Proposals 2 and 3, chiefly: segments-with-origin zoom track and merge API, unknown-kind round-trip plus reserved event shapes, appFocus/windowGeometry in v1, Cut provenance, VideoToolbox rate-control keys, a per-frame index with display time, integer-nanosecond times, cutscene-tool, click anchoring, pixel-perfect zoom level, beginActivity with latencyCritical, and UndoManager value snapshots.

## Skeptic findings

- [major] Window mode will capture menus, popovers and sheets because it sets includeChildWindows = true on a desktopIndependentWindow filter (section 2, mode table; PLAN 2.4).
- [minor] sourceReadOnlyPixelBuffer(byTrackID:) and the AVAssetWriterInputPixelBufferAdaptor replacement inputPixelBufferReceiver are "not declared anywhere in this SDK's headers or Swift interfaces" (verified list, unverified list, section 6, PLAN 1.9).
- [minor] expectsMediaDataInRealTime = true on the capture writer's video input "puts the encoder in real-time mode" (SampleWriter settings table).
- [minor] Colour management: "Capture pinned to sRGB and tagged 709 (420v with the 709 matrix) ... the writer tag 709 (AVVideoColorPropertiesKey), so VideoToolbox performs no transfer conversion" is presented as settled (section 5, Colour management).
- [minor] The pixel-perfect auto zoom rule gives 2.0x for a 4K capture exported at 1080p (section 4, Zoom level; PLAN 1.6 acceptance ".auto level is 2.0 for a 4K content on a 1080p canvas with default padding").
- [minor] Audio writer inputs take their channel count "from the first sample's format (mic usually mono, system stereo)" while the writer session starts at t0, the first video frame (section 2, SampleWriter settings and Session and clock).
- [minor] events.jsonl begins with a header line carrying t0 and every event's t is relative to t0 (section 3, Event log; InputTracker/EventLog API).
- [minor] The capture module's existing writer code is replaced cleanly: RecordingSpike.swift is deleted in task 0.4 and SampleWriter is written from scratch in task 1.1 (section 8 Capture mapping; PLAN 1.1, 3.1b).
- [major] Skipping idle frames while relying on "the renderer holds the last frame" loses the video for any static tail of a recording: the screen track's media ends one frame after the last pixel change, and CompositionBuilder's insert of keptRanges() = [0, duration] has no screen frames for the remainder, so the export shows background plus an empty card while audio continues.
- [major] The camera path as written cannot work: CameraRecorder starts during the countdown and the text says "frames before t0 are edited out", which requires appending camera frames before startSession(at: t0) can be called (t0 is only known when the first screen frame arrives, after the countdown). Appending before a session is an error, and the only alternative, retaining pre-t0 camera buffers until t0, starves AVCaptureVideoDataOutput's buffer pool.
- [major] The project and kit formats lose data across builds: additive optional fields deliberately do not bump formatVersion, synthesized Codable drops unknown keys on decode, and EditorModel autosaves (debounced) project.json, so an older build that opens a newer project or kit silently strips every field it does not know on the first save. The "older build never destroys data written by a newer one" guarantee exists only for InputEvent.unknown and Callout.unknown, not for Project, Timeline or BrandKit.
- [major] The plan's "every task is one engineer-day or less" rule is broken by the tasks that fold a one-day spike into implementation, so the M1a (~9 days) and M1 (~14 days) figures and the "first styled export around day 13" date are understated by at least two to four days.
- [minor] The event log needs t0 before any event can be written, but t0 is defined by the first .complete screen frame, which arrives asynchronously after startCapture(); the format puts t0 in the first JSONL line and EventLog.init takes the header at creation, so cursor samples taken before the first frame have no origin. PLAN 0.5 is marked independent of 0.4 although its header and every `t` depend on 0.4's t0.
- [minor] Event order in events.jsonl is not guaranteed: cursor polls (capture queue) and clicks/scroll (main thread) reach the EventLog actor through separate unstructured Tasks, and Swift actors do not guarantee FIFO across tasks; EventTrace binary-searches by time, so an unsorted read gives wrong lookups silently.
- [minor] "expectsMediaDataInRealTime = true (puts the encoder in real-time mode)" is not what the header says; the capture writer therefore runs the HEVC encoder with kVTCompressionPropertyKey_RealTime unset (unknown), and the export writer likewise never declares offline mode.
- [minor] Tagging the capture writer with AVVideoColorPropertiesKey 709/709/709 may force a per-frame color conversion rather than "no transfer conversion": if ScreenCaptureKit tags the 420v buffers with the sRGB transfer function (the stream is pinned to kCGColorSpaceSRGB), the writer converts every frame to match the 709 tags.
- [minor] The architecture states that sourceReadOnlyPixelBuffer(byTrackID:) is "not declared anywhere in this SDK's headers or Swift interfaces" and that the AVAssetWriterInputPixelBufferAdaptor replacement inputPixelBufferReceiver "is not declared in these headers"; both are declared in this SDK's Swift interface with macOS 26.0 availability. The conclusion (use the old APIs on the 15.0 floor) stands, but the unverified markers and spike items are wrong.
- [minor] The .auto zoom acceptance criterion contradicts the formula: with default padding, 4K content on a 1080p canvas resolves to 2.25, not the 2.0 that task 1.6 requires.
- [minor] "The team records on day four" and "critical path 4 days" hold only with two engineers; the plan's own dependencies make the first menu-bar recording day 6 for the single engineer the effort figures assume.
- [minor] M0's outcome promises a "crash-tolerant" recording that will "survive a crash", but the bootstrap RecordingOutputSink offers no crash-safety control and the only mechanism in the design (movieFragmentInterval) arrives with the own writer in M1; whether a kill -9 leaves a playable screen.mov in M0 is untested.
- [minor] "Editing rebuilds an immutable RenderPlan (pure Engine code, ~1 ms for a 10-minute trace)" on the @MainActor EditorModel is optimistic by one to two orders of magnitude; per-slider-tick rebuilds will jank the inspector.
- [minor] Preview and export are not mutually exclusive, yet both compositor instances funnel through RenderContext.shared under one Mutex with commandBuffer.waitUntilCompleted() held inside the lock, so an export running while the editor plays serializes every frame of each behind the other's GPU round trip.

## Changes made in the revision

- Window mode (major): the default is now a display-bound filter, SCContentFilter(display:including: [window]), where includeChildWindows applies and defaults to YES (SCStream.h:353); three strategies are defined (.displaySurface default, .pinnedFrame with sourceRect, .independentWindow = desktopIndependentWindow as the explicit 'window only, even when occluded, no menus' option) plus an Application mode for v1.1. The geometry consequence (surface = display size, SCStream.h:21) is handled by recording SCStreamFrameInfoBoundingRect (14.0) per frame ('br' in frames.jsonl) and cropping the card in ContentMapper; S4 now tests menus, popovers and sheets on all three filters and picks the default from measured size/time.
- Static-tail loss (major): AssetWriterSink appends an EmptyMedia + EndsPreviousSampleDuration marker sample at stop (AVAssetWriterInput.h rule 1, CoreMedia 10.7) and re-appends a retimed copy of the last complete frame on a 1 s idle heartbeat (CMSampleBufferCreateCopyWithNewTiming), which also lets the 2 s keyframe duration cap fire on static content; both marked unverified where the header is silent, with fallbacks. MetalCompositor additionally keeps a hold-last-frame blit copy so a request without a screen frame inside a kept range never renders an empty card (covers SCRecordingOutput tails and gaps). New acceptance in 1.1/1.2a/1.2b/1.8b/1.9/3.6: video track duration equals audio duration within one frame.
- Camera alignment (major): CameraRecorder no longer appends or retains pre-t0 frames (AVCaptureVideoDataOutput pool caution); the capture session starts during the countdown for warm-up only, startWriting happens then, and startSession(at: t0 in the session clock) is called on the first frame with PTS >= t0, relying on the header-verified empty leading edit. Task 2.1 acceptance rewritten (first camera sample within one camera frame after t0, no retained pre-t0 buffers).
- Format forward compatibility (major): Project, Timeline, ZoomTrack, ZoomSegment, CameraTrack, CaptionTrack, ExportSettings, RecordingManifest, BrandKit and its groups adopt PreservesUnknownKeys with an extra: [String: JSONValue] captured from allKeys and re-emitted on encode; EditorModel never saves an unedited project; project.json.previous is written once per editing session (the opened version). New fixtures Tests/Fixtures/projects/future and kits/future with a load -> edit -> save test; Migrator semantics unchanged (additive fields never bump).
- Plan sizing (major): split 1.2 into 1.2a (S1 spike) and 1.2b (AssetWriterSink default), 1.8 into 1.8a (S2 spike) and 1.8b (composition path), 0.6 into 0.6a (menu-bar shell with Record/Stop menu items, which is the first recording loop) and 0.6b (HUD and pill), 1.10 into 1.10a (model + preview) and 1.10b (transport + scrubber), 0.5 into 0.5a (cursor + clicks + S3) and 0.5b (scroll, focus, geometry); 0.3 trimmed to half a day with .grantedPendingRelaunch moved to 0.7. Recomputed: one engineer records from the menu bar at the end of day 5 (day 3 with two), M0 ~8.5 days, M1a ~11 days (styled export ~day 16, ~day 11 with two engineers), M1 ~16, M2 ~10, M3 ~7; the critical path lists every task on it.
- Swift-overlay APIs (minor): replaced the two 'not declared' statements with 'declared in the AVFoundation Swift overlay at macOS 26.0' (sourceReadOnlyPixelBuffer(byTrackID:) :1171-1175, AVAssetWriterInput.PixelBufferReceiver :1239-1242, inputPixelBufferReceiver :1266); removed the moot spike item; future adoption is an if #available(macOS 26, *) branch; task 0.8 records the three availabilities in platform-notes.
- Encoder real-time mode (minor): kVTCompressionPropertyKey_RealTime = true (tunable capture.realTime, optional MaximumRealTimeFrameRate = fps at 15.0) added to WriterSettings.screen/camera and RealTime = false to ExportWriterSettings.video; the expectsMediaDataInRealTime row now says 'readiness accounting'; S1 compares drop counts with the key on and off; header text recorded in 0.8.
- Colour management (minor): the 'no transfer conversion' claim is marked unverified; the capture writer omits AVVideoColorPropertiesKey (output tagged from the source, AVVideoSettings.h:156), the composition copies the written screen track's colour triple (AVVideoComposition.h recipe), and only the export writer forces 709 (tunable export.colorTags = .bt709 | .source; AVVideoTransferFunction_IEC_sRGB exists at 15.0). S1 dumps CVBufferCopyAttachments of the first complete frame and the track extensions into platform-notes.
- Auto zoom level (minor): the worked example and task 1.6 acceptance now read 2.25 for 4K content on a 1080p canvas with 0.06 per-side padding (card height 950.4 px, 2160/950.4 = 2.273 -> 2.25), with 2.0 at zero padding; padding is defined per side in the kit table; ZoomLevelTests pins the example and the rule's precision caveat is stated.
- Audio input formats (minor): AudioInputPlan resolves formats before startCapture (microphone channel count from AVCaptureDevice(uniqueID:)?.activeFormat.formatDescription clamped to <= 2 or with AVChannelLayoutKey; system audio from the configured 48 kHz stereo; all AVLinearPCM*Keys set), every input is created before startWriting, the pre-roll buffer is for timing only, and S1/1.1 gain mono, stereo and 4-channel-interface cases.
- Event log origin and ordering (minor): EventLog buffers events in memory until ScreenFrameIndexer publishes ClockOrigin(t0) on the first complete frame, then writes the header and buffered lines; negative t is legal, skipped by the engine, and tested; the file is documented as append-ordered and EventLogReader sorts stably by t with ties in file order (fixture with interleaved out-of-order lines); PLAN 0.5a depends on 0.3's ClockOrigin with a fake-origin harness instead of on 0.4's stream.
- Existing code fates (minor): a table in section 2 and task 1.1 state that RecordingWriter.swift becomes SampleWriter (keeping the fragmented .mov, marker sidecar and passthrough finalize for 3.1b), VideoEncoder.swift is kept as SampleWriter.VideoPath.vtPassthrough for an S1 A/B against .assetWriterCompression with the loser deleted in 1.2b, WriterSpike.swift stays as the S1 harness, RecordingSpike.swift is deleted in 0.4, InputTelemetryRecorder.swift evolves into InputTracker in 0.5a, CursorShapeSampler.swift becomes CursorShapeProbe in 3.5, and Core's EventLog/InputEvent are replaced in 0.1/0.2.
- M0 crash claim (minor): the M0 outcome now says events, frames and manifest survive a crash by construction while screen.mov's survival with SCRecordingOutput is measured by a kill -9 test added to 0.4's acceptance and reflected in 0.7's recovery copy.
- RenderPlan cost (minor): the '~1 ms' figure is replaced by an estimate of tens of milliseconds with the derivation, the build runs off the main actor as a nonisolated function returning sending RenderPlan with latest-wins coalescing and a 50 ms debounce, only kept ranges are precomputed, and cutscene-tool plan bench measures it (1.6 and 1.13 acceptance, risk 8).
- Render contention (minor): RenderContext.shared holds only immutable objects (device, library, pipelines) with a Mutex for lazy creation; each MetalCompositor owns a CompositorScratch (command queue, texture cache, mip and hold textures) and waits on its own command buffers outside any shared lock; S2 checks two concurrent instances, S5 benchmarks with the preview playing and may enable preview.pauseDuringExport.
- Verified-facts preamble and platform-notes task 0.8 updated with every header text cited by the reviewers (SCStream.h window-filter scoping, AVAssetWriterInput last-sample rules and channel-layout rule, VT RealTime default, AVVideoSettings colour rules and IEC_sRGB 15.0, CoreMedia marker attachments, AVCaptureVideoDataOutput pool caution, AVCaptureDevice format lookup, updateConfiguration/backgroundColor), and the unverified list rewritten to match the remaining spikes.

## Open questions

- Capture video path (spike S1, task 1.2a): do VideoToolbox rate-control keys (AverageBitRate, DataRateLimits, MaxKeyFrameInterval/Duration, AllowFrameReordering, RealTime) take effect for HEVC when passed through AVAssetWriter's AVVideoCompressionPropertiesKey? The existing VideoEncoder.swift + passthrough path (.vtPassthrough) is already confirmed on this machine; S1 A/Bs both on the same feed and the loser is deleted in 1.2b.
- Static tail (spike S1): does a compressing AVAssetWriterInput honour an EmptyMedia + EndsPreviousSampleDuration marker sample, and does a retimed copy of a retained SCK sample buffer stay unchanged while its surface is pooled? Fallbacks: re-append the last frame at stop - 1/fps, and copy the pixel buffer for the heartbeat.
- Colour tags (spike S1): what primaries/transfer/matrix attachments does ScreenCaptureKit put on 420v buffers pinned to kCGColorSpaceSRGB with the 709 matrix? Decides whether the export's forced 709 triple implies one conversion per exported frame and whether export.colorTags should default to .source.
- Fragmented .mov as the editor's source (spike S1): if AVURLAsset load or zero-tolerance seeks on a 10-minute fragmented file exceed 1 s / 150 ms, wire task 3.1b (the passthrough AVAssetExportSession finalize inherited from RecordingWriter, keeping the fragmented original until it succeeds).
- Clock bases (spikes S3/S1): confirm NSEvent.timestamp's systemUptime calibration against the stopwatch recording, and whether SCStream.synchronizationClock is the host clock or needs a stable CMSyncConvertTime offset.
- M0 recordings made with SCRecordingOutput: the 0.4 kill -9 test decides the honest recovery copy for M0; S1 measures the derived movieTimeOrigin residual and whether the video track ends early (screen.tail = .short); decide whether M0 recordings are re-aligned per recording or marked 'bootstrap format, approximate alignment' once AssetWriterSink lands.
- Window-mode default strategy (spike S4, task 2.4): .displaySurface (display-sized surface, menus and moves handled by construction, larger decode cost) versus .pinnedFrame (small files, clipped child windows, fixed-frame warning); and whether includeChildWindows actually covers menu-bar menus, context menus, popovers and sheets for display:including: filters, or whether an Application mode (display:including: [app]) is needed for menu-heavy demos.
- Preview re-render path (spike S2, task 1.8a): does a paused AVPlayerItem re-compose when videoComposition is reassigned, or is a zero-tolerance seek needed? If the round-trip exceeds ~50 ms per debounced slider change, escalate to AVPlayerItemVideoOutput + CAMetalLayer, which requires verifying that a request's source pixel buffer can be retained after finish.
- Multichannel microphones (spike S1): when an audio interface reports more than two channels, does the LPCM writer input downmix to the clamped channel count, or must the track carry the full count with AVChannelLayoutKey? Decides AudioInputPlan's clamp rule.
- Unknown-field preservation: the chosen design keeps unknown keys through extra: [String: JSONValue], which normalizes number formatting of foreign values (1 vs 1.0). Is JSON-equality (not byte-equality) acceptable for fields written by other builds, or should JSONValue keep the lexical number form? (Team decision; affects git diffs of shared kits.)
- Plan build time: the estimate is tens of milliseconds for a 10-minute trace; cutscene-tool plan bench (1.13) replaces it with a number. If it exceeds ~100 ms, precompute per segment lazily.
- System audio default: off in this design (M0's SCRecordingOutput mixes mic and system into one AAC track). Does the team want system audio on by default once AssetWriterSink separates the tracks?
- Multi-take markers and retake stitching: the research brief ranks it the number-one DevRel differentiator; DECISIONS.md v1 excludes it. A 'Marker' button in the recording pill writing marker events is cheap (half a day) and makes later stitching possible; include it in v1 or not? (User decision.)
- Keystroke capture in v1: deferred behind Input Monitoring. Enabling it earlier (opt-in) starts collecting the data the code/terminal-aware effects need, at the cost of a second TCC prompt and a secure-input redaction design. (User decision.)
- Studio Sound (noise suppression, loudness normalization) and a headless record/export CLI: the research brief puts both in v1; DECISIONS.md does not. This plan ships cutscene-tool for data only and no audio processing. Confirm or re-scope. (User decision.)
- Default zoom level: card-native .auto (2.25 for 4K on 1080p at default padding; clamp 1.5-3.0, step 0.25) versus a fixed 2.0x is a taste decision; test both with the team on real takes once M1a exists. All planner/solver numbers are kit tunables awaiting the research sweep's effect-algorithm findings.
- Intermediate bitrate factor (0.12 bit/pixel/frame HEVC) needs tuning on text-heavy UI with a legibility test, not PSNR; the research sweep's codec findings should set the balanced tier's factor and decide whether the ProRes 422 HQ 'pristine' tier is worth exposing (about 5 GB/min at 3024x1964@60).
- Cursor shapes (task 3.5) depend on the deprecated NSCursor.currentSystemCursor, which Apple says will return nil in a future macOS. Ship it as an optional feature with the arrow fallback, or skip it in v1?
- The periodic 'bypass the system private window picker' alert for app-built content filters (research brief; unverified on this machine): observe its interval during M0 dogfooding; if it is frequent, decide whether to adopt SCContentSharingPicker for display/window modes (reading its filter back needs macOS 15.2's includedWindows, above the 15.0 floor).
- Default 1080p export codec: H.264 High for compatibility (16 Mb/s) versus HEVC for size (10 Mb/s). Team preference.
- App shell preference: menu-bar agent (LSUIElement, Dock icon only while editing) is assumed; confirm versus a regular Dock app.
